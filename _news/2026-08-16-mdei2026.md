        <p class="mt-4 text-left">
          Modelware will present the paper
          <em>Beyond Syntax: Method-Compliant AI Assistance for Low-Resource Modeling Languages</em>, written with
          Sokhna Amar and Sambit Bhattacharya, at
          <a href="https://mde-intelligence.github.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">MDE Intelligence 2026</a>, the
          8th Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Model-driven Engineering. The workshop is co-located with
          <a href="https://conf.researchr.org/home/models-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener">MODELS 2026</a> in
          Málaga, Spain, in October 2026.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          Coding assistants are trained on the languages the internet is full of. Modeling languages are not those
          languages: the public corpus is thin, and an assistant that has only seen a little of one tends to produce
          output that parses but does not follow the method behind the language. Syntactic correctness is the easy
          half of the problem.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          The paper takes up the harder half, and the question of how to measure it. It presents an evaluation
          framework and a protocol-driven architecture in which the language model interprets intent while every edit
          is routed through validated <a href="/oml-code/components/mcp-server">MCP</a> tools, the same write
          operations the OML Code IDE uses, with method compliance enforced by a SHACL shape catalog encoding the rules of
          the Sierra systems engineering methodology, a DSL defined within
          <a href="/oml-code">OML</a>. Tasks are drawn from the public FireForce VI aerospace case study across three
          difficulty tiers and scored on syntax accuracy, method compliance, and API cost.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          The headline finding: tool augmentation helps most at the higher difficulty levels, which is to say
          deterministic scaffolding matters most exactly where probabilistic generation is least reliable. We wrote
          about what that means in practice in
          <a href="/syntax-is-the-easy-half">Syntax Is the Easy Half</a>.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          Accepted workshop papers are published by ACM in the joint MODELS workshop proceedings.
        </p>

        </p>
