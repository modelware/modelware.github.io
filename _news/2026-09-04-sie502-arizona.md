          <h4 class="mt-2">Congratulations to our first 14 Neurosymbolic Systems Engineering practitioners</h4>
          <p>
            The first cohort of SIE-502 has completed the course. Over eight weeks,
            14 participants went well beyond learning the concepts: each one developed
            a complete engineering methodology for an application domain of their
            choice, with its own ontologies, modeling patterns, viewpoints, and analyses.
          </p>
          <p>
            To make their methodology executable, each participant also developed
            markdown notebooks that construct models as instances of its patterns,
            answer its analysis questions, and visualize the results. They then put
            that methodology to work, using it to model a system, run engineering
            analyses on it, and answer questions that matter in their domain. The
            most rewarding part was watching participants move from
            creating models to building methodologies that make engineering knowledge
            computable, analyzable, and reusable.
          </p>
          <p class="mb-0">
            The cohort also shaped the course itself. Their projects and direct
            feedback let us develop and refine the material against real engineering
            work, and every future offering will be better for it. Thank you to all
            14 of you.
          </p>
        </div>
        <p class="mt-4 text-left">
          Modelware taught this graduate course at the
          <a href="https://sie.engineering.arizona.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Department of Systems and Industrial Engineering</a>
          at the University of Arizona in Fall 2026. To our knowledge it is one of the first anywhere to treat
          ontological modeling as a core systems engineering competency rather
          than as a specialist topic in knowledge representation.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          The premise is one we have argued for a while: a model that a machine can
          reason on is a different kind of artifact from a model that can only be
          drawn and reviewed. The course taught the semantic web stack as working
          engineering technology rather than as background theory, with
          <a href="https://www.opencaesar.io/oml/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OML</a>
          for authoring engineering ontologies, RDF as the underlying representation
          of linked knowledge, SPARQL for querying and analyzing a model, and OWL and
          SHACL for reasoning and validation.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          Coursework was hands-on, and students ran this stack themselves rather than
          reading about it. They authored ontologies, queried their own models, and put
          reasoners to work on them, so an inconsistency was something they hit and
          had to explain rather than something described in a lecture. That
          experience is hard to get any other way: the gap between a model that looks
          right and a model that holds up under inference is not obvious until a
          reasoner shows you.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          In the course project, students built their own systems engineering
          methodology and then used it to model and analyze a system of their choosing.
          Having to live with a methodology you defined yourself is the fastest way we
          know to learn what makes one good.
        </p>
        <p class="text-left">
          Teaching this material has a way of exposing which parts of a practice are
          genuinely hard. Students picked up the syntax quickly; what takes real work
          is deciding what a concept means precisely enough that a reasoner can act
          on it, and that difficulty is the discipline itself rather than an obstacle
          to it. It is also the part that
          <a href="/the-case-for-neurosymbolic-systems-engineering">Neurosymbolic Systems Engineering</a>
          makes newly practical. Once the vocabulary is fixed, an
          <a href="/oml-code/capabilities/ai-integration">AI assistant</a>
          can carry much of the formalization burden, and it does so accurately
          because it is grounded in the methodology and the model rather than
          guessing from general knowledge: the ontologies and patterns tell it what
          is valid to say, and the reasoner checks what it proposes.
        </p>
